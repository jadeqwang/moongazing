# Research: the mission assignment (shot 3.5)

*Research pass, 2026-10-07, for the revision note at 0:58: "she opens an envelope that says MISSION ASSIGNMENT on it;
you're not expected to read the letter but we've populated it in case someone pauses." Reference images are in
`media/refs/assignment/` (see `SOURCES.md` there).*

## Summary

- No real astronaut learns of a flight from a letter. They are told in person or by phone by the head of their
  astronaut office, and the paper comes afterwards. The envelope is defensible only as **the formal copy that
  follows the call**, and the script note should say so (wording in section 5).
- The real paper is a plain internal memorandum: office at the top, reference number, date, TO / FROM / SUBJECT, a few
  numbered paragraphs, a signature, a "cc" list. It is short and dry. The prop below follows that form.
- **Two things to decide before the prop is built** (section 6): `docs/identity.md` gives the agency no written name
  and forbids one on screen, so the letterhead uses the emblem plus an office line; and the launch date in the letter
  is 8 August 2037, chosen to fit the script's own calendar.

## 1. How real astronauts learn of a flight assignment

### NASA
- **Who decides.** The Chief of the Astronaut Office decides, with the concurrence of the directors of the Flight
  Operations Directorate and of Johnson Space Center. The process is written down in an internal document,
  CB-QMS-003, "Flight Operations Directorate, Crew Assignment Process", Revision B (March 2019). Source: NASA Office of
  Inspector General, "NASA's Management of Its Astronaut Corps", IG-22-007, 11 Jan 2022, pp. 22–23
  ([PDF](https://oig.nasa.gov/docs/IG-22-007.pdf)).
- **The six steps** (the report's Figure 8, saved as `03_nasa_crew_assignment_process_fig8_oig_ig22007_p23.jpg`):
  1. the Chief Astronaut receives notice of the "Latest Crew Assignment Date";
  2. reviews training and performance records with other Astronaut Office officials;
  3. submits a crew assignment proposal to the Flight Operations Directorate and Johnson management;
  4. the proposal goes to the Multilateral Crew Operations Panel for the international partners' concurrence, and the
     Chief Astronaut announces the selection at an internal all-hands Astronaut Office meeting;
  5. the Chief Astronaut notifies the directorate's Flight Integration Division to begin the crew training flow;
  6. NASA announces the crew publicly.
- **Eligibility and medicine.** An Astronaut Evaluation Board rates astronauts "eligible" for assignment. They must be
  medically qualified by the Crew Health and Safety office, and for long flights the Multilateral Space Medicine Board
  gives the final medical certification of a station crew (same report, p. 23).
- **Timing.** Station crews are assigned to an expedition about 24 months before launch, and an assignment brings
  18 to 24 months of mission-specific training. Since 2015 most single-system training is done in a
  "pre-assignment" phase, before the crew is named (same report, training section and footnote 37).
- **How the person is told.** In person or by phone, by the chief or the director:
  - *Artemis II, 2023.* The four were called to a meeting disguised on their calendars as something else. Christina
    Koch asked to join remotely and her boss replied that she would want to be there in person. Reid Wiseman was at a
    doctor's appointment; Chief Astronaut Joe Acaba texted "You have to get here", then "Dial in", and Wiseman learned
    he would command the flight while walking to his truck
    ([Washington Post via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/nasa-astronauts-learned-d-assigned-000320265.html)).
  - *Shuttle era.* Michael Coats recalls the STS-41-D crew being called to the office of George Abbey, Director of
    Flight Crew Operations, who mumbled a question about whether they still wanted to fly
    ([JSC oral history](https://historycollection.jsc.nasa.gov/JSCHistoryPortal/history/oral_histories/CoatsML/CoatsML_11-9-12.htm)).
    Bill McArthur got a phone call in 1992 from Dan Brandenstein, chief of the office, asking whether he would be
    willing to fly a medical research mission
    ([JSC oral history](https://historycollection.jsc.nasa.gov/JSCHistoryPortal/history/oral_histories/McArthurWS/McArthurWS_2-3-17.pdf)).
- **What the public sees.** A numbered news release. Artemis II was release 23-040 (3 April 2023), with the position
  titles exactly as "Commander Reid Wiseman, Pilot Victor Glover, Mission Specialist 1 Christina Hammock Koch, and
  Mission Specialist 2 Jeremy Hansen", and quotes from the Johnson director and the Director of Flight Operations
  ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-names-astronauts-to-next-moon-mission-first-crew-under-artemis/)).
  Station releases use the phrase "will launch no earlier than" with a date.
- **The call-then-paper precedent.** When Sally Ride was selected as an astronaut candidate, George Abbey phoned her on
  16 January 1978, and a Western Union Mailgram signed by a NASA personnel officer followed the same day:
  "I congratulate you on having been selected for the astronaut candidate program… You are scheduled to report for duty
  at the Johnson Space Center on July 10, 1978." She kept it all her life; it was sold from her estate in 2025
  ([collectSPACE](https://www.collectspace.com/news/news-061625a-astronaut-sally-ride-estate-collection-auction.html)).
  This is selection rather than flight assignment, but it is the clearest real case of a formal paper following the
  call and being kept.

### The International Space Station partners (the multilateral model)
- The Multilateral Crew Operations Panel (NASA, Roscosmos, JAXA, ESA, CSA) assigns crews by consensus, including who is
  station commander and who trains for spacewalks and robotics. Prime and backup crews are usually named together
  ([ESA](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Human_and_Robotic_Exploration/Astronauts/Assigning_an_astronaut_to_a_mission)).
  Its founding document is "Principles Regarding Processes and Criteria for Selection, Assignment, Training and
  Certification of ISS (Expedition and Visiting) Crewmembers", November 2001.
- **ESA.** The Director General makes the announcement, usually at a political occasion. Josef Aschbacher announced
  Sophie Adenot's and Raphaël Liégeois's first flights at the Space Council in Brussels on 22 May 2024, one month
  after their class graduated
  ([ESA press release 28-2024](https://esa.int/Newsroom/Press_Releases/ESA_astronauts_Sophie_Adenot_and_Raphael_Liegeois_assigned_for_Space_Station_missions)).
- **JAXA.** A dated press release with a comment from the agency president. For Akihiko Hoshide (2 March 2018) it gives
  the expedition numbers ("Expedition 64/65"), the role (commander of the 65th), the approval ("with the approval of
  the… Multilateral Crew Operations Panel"), the stay ("approximately six months starting around May of 2020") and the
  training start ("around autumn 2019")
  ([JAXA](https://global.jaxa.jp/press/2018/03/20180302_hoshide.html)). That is the same list of facts our letter needs.

### Russia (Roscosmos and the Gagarin Cosmonaut Training Centre)
- Crews train for years as prime and backup. Near the end they sit two days of examinations, after which an
  Interdepartmental Commission at the training centre recommends them. The final decision is made by the State
  Commission at Baikonur, usually the day before launch: for Soyuz MS-10 the commission approved both crews on the
  evening of 10 October 2018 and the launch was on the 11th
  ([inform.kz](https://www.inform.kz/en/soyuz-ms-10-crew-had-prelaunch-press-conference-at-baikonur_a3418070),
  [TASS](https://tass.com/science/879232/amp)).
- The crews attend in suits and ties, in quarantine behind glass, prime crew on one side and backup on the other
  (`05_…exp72_2024.jpg`, `06_…exp75_2026.jpg`).
- For the Vostok flights the paper was a formal "flight assignment" signed by the head of cosmonaut training and the
  chief designer: for Vostok 2 it was signed by Kamanin on 3 August and Korolev on 4 August 1961
  ([RussianSpaceWeb](https://russianspaceweb.com/vostok2.html)). Gagarin and Titov were chosen by the State Commission
  on 8 April 1961, four days before the flight ([Vostok 1, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vostok_1)).

### China (China Manned Space Agency, Astronaut Centre of China)
- Crews are selected internally about a year to eighteen months before the flight, prime and backup together
  (Yang Liwei, as quoted in Chinese press; I did not pin down the original link), and named to the public the day
  before launch at a press conference at the launch site. The formula is fixed:
  "经任务总指挥部研究决定…飞行乘组由航天员蔡旭哲、宋令东、王浩泽组成，蔡旭哲担任指令长" ("By decision of the mission
  headquarters… the flight crew consists of astronauts Cai Xuzhe, Song Lingdong and Wang Haoze, with Cai Xuzhe as
  commander"), announced 29 October 2024 for a launch on the 30th
  ([China Daily, Xinhua text](https://cn.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202410/29/WS67207e49a310b59111da06d6.html)).
- The useful vocabulary: 飞行乘组 (flight crew), 指令长 (commander), 航天飞行工程师 (flight engineer), 载荷专家 (payload
  specialist), 主份 / 备份乘组 (prime / backup crew), 入选乘组 (selected into the crew).

### What this means for the prop
A real assignment document is a short internal memorandum, never a certificate. It names the mission by number, the
position, prime or backup, the commander, the launch target as "no earlier than", when and where to report, and that
the assignment depends on medical certification. It is signed by the director who owns flight crews and copied to the
chief astronaut and the flight surgeon. The person already knows by the time it arrives.

## 2. What the real documents look like

| Feature | Real practice | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Letterhead | Agency name and centre in small type at top left; emblem at top right; nothing else | `01_nasa_memo_letterhead_oig_ig22007_p32.jpg` |
| Routing line | `Reply to Attn of:` followed by an office or mail code. At Johnson, CA is the directorate and CB the Astronaut Office, which is why the process document is numbered CB-QMS-003 | same |
| Header block | `TO:` / `FROM:` / `SUBJECT:` by office title, not by personal name; older memos use "MEMORANDUM For Director / Subject:" | `01_…`, `04_nasa_stg_memo_1961_…` |
| Body | Numbered paragraphs, plain sentences, no adjectives | `04_…` |
| Position titles | Commander, Pilot, Mission Specialist 1 and 2 (Orion); Commander and Flight Engineer (station expeditions and Soyuz); 1961: "Spacecraft Commander – Pilot", "Navigator – Copilot", "Systems Manager" | NASA release 23-040; `04_…` |
| Mission numbering | Expedition numbers ("Expedition 64/65"); flight names ("Artemis II", "Crew-12", "Soyuz MS-10") | JAXA 2018 |
| Launch date | "no earlier than" plus a date; internal papers write NET, and count days as L-minus | NASA station releases |
| Medical | Certification by a medical board is a condition, stated in one sentence | OIG report p. 23 |
| Signature | Today a small digital-signature stamp at the left margin above the typed name; before that, ink | `02_nasa_memo_signature_block_…` |
| Distribution | A "cc:" list, or a distribution list by title | OIG report Appendix C |
| Markings | Rubber stamps top and bottom when restricted (1961: CONFIDENTIAL) | `04_…` |

## 3. The envelope (prop text)

A C5 or US letter-size envelope in heavy off-white stock, the paper of a university admission letter. The front must
read in under a second, so it carries one large line and almost nothing else.

```
[emblem, 14 mm]  FLIGHT CREW OPERATIONS                                   (top left, small caps, 7 pt)



                         MISSION ASSIGNMENT                               (centre, letterspaced serif caps, 22 pt)
                            飞行任务通知书                                  (directly under it, 11 pt)



                         Dr. Jade Wang                                    (addressee block, 11 pt)
                         Astronaut Office


REF FCO-36-0147 · GH-E1                        PERSONAL — ADDRESSEE ONLY  (bottom edge, monospaced, 6 pt)
```

- **Emblem:** `media/chars/identity/emblem_final_small.svg` at top left, about 14 mm, in its own indigo and gold.
  No red anywhere on the front.
- **Back flap (optional, for the opening shot):** the station seal 廣寒 (`seal_guanghan.png`, 白文) stamped across the
  flap edge as a closing seal, so that her thumb breaks it. `docs/identity.md` already assigns this seal to the 3.5
  letter. It is the only red on the object.
- **Reference number:** office code, two-digit year, serial (FCO = Flight Crew Operations, 2036, memo 147), modelled
  on NASA's mail-code numbering. GH-E1 = Guanghan, Expedition 1.
- **Handling line:** "PERSONAL — ADDRESSEE ONLY" is the ordinary marking on personnel mail. Avoid "CONFIDENTIAL" or
  "TOP SECRET": a crew assignment is announced to the press within days.
- **The Chinese line.** Use **飞行任务通知书** (short form 任务通知书). 通知书 is the standard name for an official
  notice delivered to one person: 录取通知书 (university admission notice), 入伍通知书 (enlistment notice). A
  Chinese-reading viewer will feel the admission-letter echo at once, which is the right emotion for the shot.
  任务指派 is the literal translation of "task assignment"; it is a verb phrase from project management and is not the
  title of a document. 任命书 means appointment to an office and would suit only the commander.
- No street address, no stamp, no postmark: it is delivered by hand or by internal courier.

## 4. The letter (prop text)

One page, the emblem at top right (as on the NASA memo), black serif type, generous margins. English text: 194 words.

```
FLIGHT CREW OPERATIONS                                                              [emblem]
GUANGHAN STATION PROGRAMME

MEMORANDUM                                                                  Ref: FCO-36-0147
                                                                                 9 June 2036

TO:        Dr. Jade Wang, Astronaut Office
FROM:      Director, Flight Crew Operations
SUBJECT:   Crew Assignment — Guanghan Station, Expedition 1 (GH-E1)

1.  On the recommendation of the Chief, Astronaut Office, and with the concurrence of the
    Crew Operations Panel, you are assigned to the prime crew of Expedition 1 to Guanghan
    Station (89.5°S, Shackleton–de Gerlache ridge) as Mission Specialist — ISRU & Habitat
    Systems.

2.  Expedition 1 is a crew of eight under Commander Chen Yu, launching as two crews of four.
    You are manifested on Flight E1-A.

3.  Launch: no earlier than 8 August 2037, Wenchang, on a heavy-lift crew launch vehicle;
    lunar-orbit rendezvous with the pre-positioned lander. Planned stay on station:
    approximately 13 months.

4.  Report to Crew Training at 0800 on Monday, 7 July 2036, to begin the assigned-crew
    training flow. Health stabilisation (quarantine) begins at L−14 days.

5.  This assignment remains subject to flight medical certification by the Space Medicine
    Board.

Congratulations, Jade. It is well earned. Go and tell your family.

王博士：经研究决定，你入选广寒站第一长期考察组主份乘组，担任任务专家（原位资源利用与居住舱系统）。
指令长：陈宇。乘组共八人。计划不早于2037年8月8日自文昌发射，驻站约十三个月。请于2036年7月7日
报到，开始任务训练。

[signature]                                                                 [廣寒 seal]
W. N. Kamau
Director, Flight Crew Operations

cc:  Chief, Astronaut Office
     Crew Flight Surgeon
     Commander, Expedition 1
```

Notes on the choices:

- **Every phrase has a real model.** "On the recommendation of the Chief, Astronaut Office" and "concurrence" are the
  NASA chain (OIG report). "Crew Operations Panel" is the Multilateral Crew Operations Panel with "Multilateral"
  dropped, since this is one agency. "Prime crew", "manifested", "no earlier than", "L−14 days", "assigned-crew
  training flow" and "Space Medicine Board" are all working terms.
- **Dates.** 9 June 2036 is a Monday, 14 months before launch. 7 July 2036 is a Monday. The launch date is explained
  in section 6. L−14 is the usual two-week pre-launch health stabilisation; for 8 August it falls on 25 July 2037.
- **Why 14 months is believable.** Real station crews are assigned about 24 months out, but most systems training now
  happens in a "pre-assignment" phase before the crew is named. The letter says she begins the *assigned-crew* flow,
  which implies she has been in the Expedition 1 training pool already. An insider will read 14 months as tight but
  normal for a first expedition.
- **The Chinese paragraph** says: "Dr. Wang: it has been decided that you are selected for the prime crew of Guanghan
  Station Expedition 1 as mission specialist (in-situ resource utilisation and habitat systems). Commander: Chen Yu.
  Crew of eight. Launch planned no earlier than 8 August 2037 from Wenchang; about thirteen months on station.
  Report on 7 July 2036 to begin mission training." 长期考察组 is the established Chinese term for a station
  "Expedition"; 主份乘组, 指令长 and 任务专家 are the terms the Chinese programme uses; "你" (not "您") is the
  form official notices use. Chen Yu is 陈宇 as in `docs/character_bible.md`. It avoids giving Jade a Chinese given
  name, since none is established.
- **The seal** sits beside the signature and date, where the red seal sits on a Chinese official document. It is the
  station's seal, so it does not break the "no red in the agency emblem" rule.
- **The last line** is typed as part of the letter. If the shot wants one more human touch, make "Go and tell your
  family." a handwritten addition in blue ink beside the signature; directors do that on letters of this kind.
- **The signatory** "W. N. Kamau" is an invented placeholder from a ninth country (Kenya), so that the director is
  not from any crew member's nation. Change it freely.

## 5. Staging realism (for the script note)

In 2036 nobody would learn of a flight from the post, and a reviewer from a real programme will know it: the news
comes as a call or a summons from the chief astronaut, as it did for the Artemis II crew in 2023 and for shuttle crews
called to George Abbey's office. The defensible framing is that **she already knows**. The chief called earlier that
day; what arrives that evening, by hand, is the formal signed copy of record, on paper because Expedition 1 is the
first crew of a new station and the programme issues the original as a keepsake, the way Sally Ride received and kept
the Mailgram that followed George Abbey's phone call in 1978. Paper also survives where a message does not: the
letter can go into her kit and to the Moon. This framing improves the beat. She is not finding out; she is holding it,
at the window, with the toy Earth in her other hand, deciding how to tell the children. Suggested script note for 3.5:
*"The formal paper copy of her assignment, hand-delivered after the chief's call. She already knows. She breaks the
seal and looks at it for a moment before she turns to the room."*

## 6. Two flags for the director

1. **The agency has no name.** The brief asked for "the agency's name as given in `docs/identity.md`", but that file
   says: "No letters or acronym. The agency has no written name on screen." The props therefore carry the emblem and
   an office line (FLIGHT CREW OPERATIONS / GUANGHAN STATION PROGRAMME), which is how real internal memos are headed
   anyway. If a name is wanted, it has to be decided first; nothing here invents one.
2. **The launch date.** The brief said autumn 2037. The script's own calendar needs Mid-Autumn on the Moon early in
   the stay, about 13 months on station, home for the next Mid-Autumn, and a thin waning crescent before dawn at
   launch. Computed lunar phases give Mid-Autumn on 24 September 2037 and 13 September 2038, and new moons on
   11 August and 9 September 2037.
   - **Chosen: launch 8 August 2037** (a waning crescent three days before new moon; the day after 立秋, the start of
     autumn in the Chinese calendar). Landing 12 August, Mid-Autumn on day 47, 13.2 months to the 2038 festival.
     All four constraints hold.
   - *Alternative: launch 7 September 2037.* Autumn by any calendar and Mid-Autumn falls on day 17, but the stay to
     the next Mid-Autumn is only 12 months. If this is preferred, change the letter to "7 September 2037",
     "approximately 12 months", date it 7 July 2036 and report on 3 August 2036.
   - Note that `docs/audit_claude.md` worked an earlier calendar (launch 30 August 2035, home 4 October 2036). The
     2037 dates here replace it only if the film's year is now 2037.
