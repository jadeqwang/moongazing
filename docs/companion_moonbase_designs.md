## 1. NASA Moon Base (2026). Successor to "Artemis Base Camp"

**What it is.** The March and May 2026 plans turn the 2020 "Artemis Base Camp" concept into a funded, phased programme. The phase table names the actual hardware:

- **Phase 1 (now–2029):** Blue Moon Mk1 *Endurance* (Shackleton Connecting Ridge), Griffin-1, IM-3 *Trinity*, **VIPER** (late 2027), CAPSTONE 02, Blue Ghost M2 with **LuSEE-Night** on the far side, **MoonFall** (four rocket-propelled survey drones, 2028), and the unpressurized rovers **Pegasus** (Lunar Outpost, ~9 mph+) and **CLV-1** (Astrolab, ~6 mph+, folding).
- **Phase 2 (2029–2032):** power, logistics, comms, and the **JAXA/Toyota pressurized rover**.
- **Phase 3 (2032+):** permanent habitats, nuclear and solar power, comms network, lunar GPS.

Sources: [NASA Moon Base Phases](https://www.nasa.gov/moonbase-phases/), [Sky at Night on the May 26 event](https://www.skyatnightmagazine.com/news/nasa-moon-base-announcement-may-2026).

**Visual.** NASA's official phase renderings show a **spread-out** base, not a single dome:

- low cylindrical modules and landers sitting on gray, sun-raked south-pole terrain;
- vertical solar arrays on tall masts (the Sun is always near the horizon, so panels stand **upright** and rotate to track it);
- rovers on graded roads;
- landing zones set well apart from the habitats.

The palette is white and silver hardware, gold MLI foil on landers, and neutral-gray regolith. Shadows are long and black.

### 1a. Artemis Base Camp (the 2020 concept)
NASA's 2020 report to the National Space Council ([PDF](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/a_sustained_lunar_presence_nspc_report4220final.pdf)) defined three core surface elements: the **Foundation Surface Habitat**, the **Lunar Terrain Vehicle (LTV)**, and the **Habitable Mobility Platform** (pressurized rover). Supporting infrastructure would include power, comms, radiation shielding, a **landing pad**, waste disposal and storage ([Universe Today](https://www.universetoday.com/articles/nasas-plans-for-a-lunar-base-camp)).

### 1b. Foundation Surface Habitat (FSH)
- **Shape and scale:** a **three-storey vertical stack**. The bottom storey is a rigid metal cylinder about **4 m (13 ft) wide**. Above it, a **two-storey inflatable**, about **6.5 m (21 ft)** in diameter, bulges outward. Total habitable volume is about **127 m³**, roughly a tenth of the ISS. Mass must stay **under 12 t**, and it lands on its own lander stage.
- **Crew:** 2 people for 30 days at first, growing to 4 for 60 days.
- **Layout:** Floor 1 holds the airlock, suit storage, a geology glovebox and O₂ generation. Floor 2 holds hygiene, the biology lab, exercise and an **observation window**. Floor 3 holds crew quarters, medical, the galley and labs.
- **Look:** white woven outer skin (multi-layer, "stronger than Kevlar") over a metallic base, sitting high on lander legs. One real window. *For the homesick shot: the observation window is on floor 2.*
- Source: [AmericaSpace](https://www.americaspace.com/2024/01/13/living-on-the-moon-inside-artemis-foundation-habitat/).

### 1c. Lunar Terrain Vehicles (unpressurized)
- **What:** an open "moon buggy" for two suited astronauts, which can also be driven remotely between crews. The May 2026 awards went to **Astrolab** ($219M, Crewed Lunar Vehicle / FLEX lineage) and **Lunar Outpost** ($220M, *Pegasus*).
- **Look:** golf-cart to small-SUV size. Open chassis, **mesh or airless tyres**, high-gain antenna mast, white and silver body. Astrolab's design has a **cargo deck and a robotic arm**. Crew ride **standing or semi-reclined**; seats have no backs like car seats because of the PLSS backpacks.
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/ee/Lunar_Terrain_Vehicle_2022.png · Pegasus: https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/pegasus-img-6768.png

### 1d. Pressurized rover: JAXA/Toyota "Lunar Cruiser"
- **Scale:** about **6.0 × 5.2 × 3.8 m** (a minibus), with roughly 13 m³ of living space. Two crew live in shirtsleeves for up to **30 days**. Six wheels. Solar power plus Toyota **regenerative fuel cells**.
- **Look:** rounded white cab with large wraparound windows, a **suitport/airlock at the rear**, and big six-wheel stance. NASA lists it under Moon Base Phase 2.
- Sources: [NASA Moon Base Phases](https://www.nasa.gov/moonbase-phases/), [Toyota Times](https://toyotatimes.jp/en/toyota_news/1039.html). Image: https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/20231028-01.jpg

## 3. China–Russia International Lunar Research Station (ILRS, 国际月球科研站)

- **Plan:** Stage 1 (2026–2030) is Chang'e-7 and -8 plus Russia's Luna-28. Stage 2 (2031–2035) is the dedicated missions **ILRS-1 to -5**, giving the "basic model" at the south pole by about 2035. The "extended model" (about 2050) is a network: a **lunar-orbit station as hub**, the **south-pole station as main base**, and nodes on the equator and far side. A robotically emplaced **nuclear power plant** is planned for 2033–35. China's "555 Project" aims for 50 countries, 500 institutions and 5,000 researchers. Sources: [Space.com](https://space.com/china-moon-base-south-pole-2035), [New Space Economy](https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2025/06/30/chinas-international-lunar-research-station-what-is-it-and-why-is-it-important/), [Space.com: reactor](https://www.space.com/russia-china-shared-nuclear-reactor-2035-moon).
- **Visual (from CNSA renderings):** robotics-first. Clusters of **cylindrical modules and landers**, a central command node, **robotic arms and robots**, rovers and hoppers, large solar arrays, and a relay satellite overhead. Gold foil and white panels are common, as on Chang'e landers. Chang'e-8's **robot stacking 3D-printed regolith bricks** is a perfect "speed-build" visual.
- **Image ref:** member map https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2b/International_Lunar_Research_Station_Members.svg. CNSA concept art appears in the [UNOOSA 2024 presentation (PDF)](https://www.unoosa.org/documents/pdf/copuos/stsc/2024/ListTechnicalPresentations/2024_STSC_technical_presentations_slides/6_--_Chinas_deep_Space_exploration_Mr.YANG_Ruihong_China_pres.pdf).

> **Accuracy flag for the story:** U.S. law (the 2011 "Wolf Amendment") bars NASA from bilateral cooperation with China. In the real 2030s, Artemis and ILRS are **two separate camps**. An "international crew" on a NASA base realistically means U.S., Canada, ESA nations, Japan, UAE and others. A shared US–China base would be a deliberate **fictional/aspirational** choice. That's fine, but make it an intentional choice (for example, one Earth-wide agency, as in the treatment).

## 4. ESA "Moon Village" (SOM + ESA + MIT)

- **What:** Skidmore, Owings & Merrill worked with ESA and MIT (2019 onward) on a design for the first permanent settlement, on the **rim of Shackleton crater**.
- **Look:** clusters of **3–4-storey semi-inflatable pods**. Each is a rigid composite frame (three columns) plus an **inflatable shell** that roughly **doubles its volume** after landing, and each is wrapped in a protective outer shell against temperature, impacts, dust and radiation. The interiors are open and loft-like, with no central core. Pods are clustered near the crater's ice and joined by links, and **comms towers stand on the highest ridges**. Each module houses 4–6 people.
- **Palette:** off-white, softly faceted shells; regolith-gray surroundings; lit windows in bands.
- Sources: [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/?p=1344775), [ESA CDF study](https://www.esa.int/Enabling_Support/Space_Engineering_Technology/CDF/ESA_engineers_assess_Moon_Village_habitat), [Universe Today](https://universetoday.com/151400/conceptual-design-for-a-lunar-habitat).
- **Image:** https://static.dezeen.com/uploads/2019/04/moon-village-som-news-architecture-design_dezeen_2364_hero-852x479.jpg

## 5. Foster + Partners / ESA 3D-printed regolith dome (2013)

- **What:** ESA with Foster + Partners and Monolite (D-Shape printer) studied printing a base from regolith at the south pole.
- **Look:**
  - An inflatable is landed first. A robot-driven printer then builds a **catenary dome** over it from regolith.
  - The dome wall is a **closed-cell foam-like lattice** ("like bird bones"), which saves mass while shielding micrometeoroids and radiation.
  - The result is **multi-dome clusters**: gray, porous, organic-looking shells with an arched entrance tube. Picture **igloos made of moon-sand coral**.
  - A **1.5 t demo block** was printed in a vacuum chamber. The printer frame is about 6 m. Printing rate is about 2 m/hour.
- Source: [ESA](https://www.esa.int/Enabling_Support/Space_Engineering_Technology/Building_a_lunar_base_with_3D_printing).
- **Images:** https://www.esa.int/var/esa/storage/images/esa_multimedia/images/2013/01/multi-dome_base_being_constructed/12502108-1-eng-GB/Multi-dome_base_being_constructed_article.jpg · https://www.esa.int/var/esa/storage/images/esa_multimedia/images/2013/01/1.5_tonne_building_block_produced_as_a_demonstration/12501046-1-eng-GB/1.5_tonne_building_block_produced_as_a_demonstration_article.jpg

## 6. ICON "Project Olympus" (with BIG, SEArch+ and NASA)

- **What:** **laser-melted regolith** construction ("Laser Vulcan"). NASA awarded a $57.2M SBIR contract running through 2028, and a small prototype is planned to print a "sidewalk tile" on the Moon. ICON flew the **Duneflow** reduced-gravity regolith experiment on Blue Origin (Feb 2025). Sources: [Freethink](https://www.freethink.com/space/building-on-the-moon), [Maker Faire / NASA summary](https://makerfairerome.eu/en/nasa-advance-lunar-construction-technology-for-moon-missions/).
- **Look (BIG renderings):**
  - **Low, vaulted, ribbed shells.** The walls are a waffle-like network of rigid exterior ribs, filled with loose regolith as radiation mass.
  - **Glassy, dark-gray/charcoal sintered surfaces**, because melted regolith is basalt-glass dark, not concrete-white.
  - The layer lines of printing are visible. Domes sit half-sunk into terrain, with laser-printed roads and pads.
  - **Construction visual:** a gantry or rover with a **glowing red-orange laser melt pool** leaving a dark glassy bead behind.
- **Images:** https://www.inexhibit.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/big-bjarke-ingels-icon-on-the-moon-cover-1.jpg · more at [Inexhibit](https://www.inexhibit.com/marker/project-olympus-the-sustainable-lunar-habitat-designed-by-big-and-icon/)

## 7. Fission Surface Power (FSP) and Kilopower

- **Kilopower / KRUSTY (2017–18):** a ground test of a ~1 kWe reactor using **Stirling engines** at the Nevada test site. The design scales to 10 kWe. [NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20180000691). Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6a/Kilopower_experiment.jpg
- **FSP 40 kWe (2022 contracts):** three ~$5M concept studies went to Lockheed/BWXT/Creare, Westinghouse/Aerojet and IX (Intuitive Machines plus X-energy)/Maxar/Boeing. The requirements were 40 kWe, under 6 t, and at least 10-year life. [NucNet](https://www.nucnet.org/news/nasa-chooses-three-companies-to-produce-concepts-for-fission-surface-power-system-7-1-2022)
- **2025–26 reset:** NASA and DOE signed an MOU (Jan 2026) for a reactor **on the surface by 2030**. It must deliver **100 kWe** with a **closed Brayton** converter and be ready to launch around late 2029 on a lander carrying up to 15 t. [World Nuclear News](https://world-nuclear-news.org/articles/nasa-doe-join-together-for-lunar-reactor-by-2030), [Aviation Week](https://aviationweek.com/space/budget-policy-regulation/nasa-aims-launch-lunar-fission-reactor-2030), [Lockheed Martin 2026](https://www.lockheedmartin.com/en-us/news/features/2026/fission-surface-power-endless-power-in-the-lunar-night.html)
- **Look:**
  - A **mast or tower** rises from a reactor that is **buried in or bermed by regolith** for shielding.
  - **Large flat radiator panels** fan out from the tower like **a tall thin tree with wide square leaves, or a sail**. They are often dark or mirrored, and they glow only in infrared, not visibly.
  - It sits **far from the habitat** (hundreds of meters to around a km), connected by a ground cable.
  - **Avoid** any visible glow, cooling towers or steam. Nothing visible happens.
- Images: https://www.nasa.gov/image-detail/fsp-beautyshot/ · https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20180000691

## 8. South-pole real estate: Shackleton, the Connecting Ridge, and peaks of eternal light

- **Shackleton crater:** about **21 km (13 mi) wide** and about **4 km deep**, roughly twice the depth of the Grand Canyon, sitting almost exactly on the pole. Its floor is in **permanent shadow** at about **−184 °C (≈90 K)**, "300 °F below zero" ([NASA SVS 4944 captions](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4944)). The rim gets near-continuous sun.
- **Connecting Ridge** joins Shackleton to de Gerlache crater. It is one of the best-lit places on the Moon: modelling finds points that are sunlit **92.3% of the time at 2 m height and 95.7% at 10 m height** ([DLR, Gläser et al.](https://elib.dlr.de/94242)). It is the target of **Moon Base I** (Blue Moon *Endurance*). Chang'e-7 targets Shackleton's **sunlit rim** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_7)).
- **"Peaks of eternal light" are a myth (almost).** At the 500 m scale **no south-pole point is permanently lit**. There are only small "islands" a few hundred meters across that are lit about 70–90% of southern winter and nearly all of summer ([Wikipedia: Peak of eternal light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_of_eternal_light), [arXiv 1608.01989](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.01989)). This is why towers matter: **raising solar panels 10 m buys several % more sunlight.**
- **Malapert massif** ("Mons Malapert"), about 85 mi from Shackleton, is a 5 km-high mountain that dominates the horizon in the Earth-facing direction. It is an Artemis candidate region and a classic **comms-relay site** because it keeps Earth in view more often.
- **Look:**
  - **Bright sunlit crests above pitch-black basins.** The sun is never more than about **1.5° above the horizon**, so ridges catch light like **islands of white in a sea of black ink** (very 水墨).
  - **Shadows hundreds of metres long.**
  - The terrain is **highlands** (anorthosite), a lighter neutral gray than the dark maria of Apollo 11.
- **Images:** annotated south pole https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/ac/Lunar_south_pole_summer_annotated.jpg · ShadowCam view *into* Shackleton https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:ShadowCam-Shackleton-Crater-768x465.png · NASA SVS Shackleton visualization https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4716/ · Earth and Sun from Shackleton rim https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a000000/a004900/a004944/shackleton_earth.0001_print.jpg

## 9. Permanently shadowed regions (PSRs)

- **What:** crater floors near the poles that **have not seen direct sunlight for billions of years**. LRO Diviner measured temperatures as low as **~25 K (−248 °C)**, among the coldest measured anywhere in the solar system ([Diviner/UCLA](https://www.diviner.ucla.edu/science), [Space.com](https://space.com/7311-moon-craters-coldest-place-solar-system.html)). They are cold traps for water ice and other volatiles.
- **Look:**
  - Not totally black. PSRs are **faintly lit by sunlight bouncing off the sunlit crater walls**. LRO's ShadowCam images show a ghostly, low-contrast, blue-gray world inside.
  - Rover headlights (VIPER has them) throw **hard-edged cones** with no beam glow in the air (there is no air to scatter it).
  - **Breath and steam don't exist outside.** Frost only shows if you dig. Surface ice is mixed into grains, not glaciers.
- Images: https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/3577 (Permanent Shadows on the Moon) · https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4043 (LRO peers into shadows)

## 10. Ice prospecting: the VIPER legacy

- **VIPER** (Volatiles Investigating Polar Exploration Rover) was **cancelled in July 2024**, nearly complete. It was **revived in Sept 2025** with a $190M contract for Blue Origin to deliver it on Blue Moon Mk1 in **late 2027**, for a ~100-day mission mapping ice with a **1 m drill (TRIDENT)**, neutron and IR spectrometers, and a mass spectrometer ([Astronomy](https://www.astronomy.com/space-exploration/nasa-revives-viper-moon-rover-taps-blue-origin-for-lunar-landing/)). Background: LCROSS (2009) found about **5.6 ± 2.9% water by mass** in the ejecta from Cabeus crater.
- **Look:** a **golf-cart-sized** four-wheel rover with a tall camera/light **mast**. It is the **first lunar rover with headlights**, needed for driving in shadow. It is silver-white with a solar panel on its side; the panels are vertical because the sun is low. The drill is on its belly.
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e6/VIPER_lunar_rover.jpg · NASA: https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/jsc2024e059358.jpg
- **China's version:** Chang'e-7's **hopper** will literally rocket-hop into a shadowed crater to sniff water with onboard analyzers.

## 11. Landing pads (sintered regolith)

- **Why:** engine plumes **sandblast** regolith at high speed. Apollo 12 landed about 180 m from Surveyor 3 and pitted its surfaces. Starship-class landers will throw much more. NASA's **SCALPSS** cameras (on Moon Base I) will film plume-surface interaction for exactly this reason.
- **Concepts:** **microwave-sintered** pads (regolith absorbs microwaves well, and magnetic beneficiation boosts absorption by 60–80%), possibly with polymer-infused outer rings ([Universe Today](https://www.universetoday.com/159427/want-to-build-structures-on-the-moon-just-blast-the-regolith-with-microwaves), [NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20220005023)), as well as laser sintering (ICON) and paver tiles.
- **Look:**
  - A **flat circular pad of darker, glassy, fused regolith**, tens of meters across, ringed by a **raised berm or blast wall**.
  - Some concepts use hexagonal interlocking pavers.
  - Pads sit **well away from habitats** (on the order of 1 km+), with a graded road leading in.
  - During landing, dust shoots out **radially in flat, straight sheets**. It does not billow.

## 12. Radiation shielding: regolith berms and bags

- **Numbers:** about **0.8 m** of regolith gives significant protection; **~2–3 m** brings exposure near terrestrial norms and handles cosmic-ray secondaries reasonably; about **5 m** is the "all-purpose" figure for radiation, thermal and micrometeorite protection together ([summary of studies](https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2026/01/12/what-will-it-take-to-live-on-the-moon/), [APS](https://meetings-archive.aps.org/ses/2019/d04/46)).
- **Look:**
  - Habitats **disappear under mounds**: rounded regolith hills, or stacked **woven sandbags** of regolith, piled over inflatable or metal cores.
  - Only airlocks, windows (if any), radiators and antennas poke out.
  - A real 2030s base looks like **a cluster of gray burial mounds with gleaming hardware entrances**, not shiny domes on bare ground.
  - Robotic bulldozers or excavators push regolith. Interlune and Vermeer have prototyped a lunar excavator that ingests about **100 t of regolith per hour** ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/interlune-excavator-helium-3-moon-construction/)).

## 13. Inflatables: Sierra Space LIFE

- **What:** the **Large Integrated Flexible Environment**, a softgoods habitat woven from **Vectran** straps. Full-scale burst tests (Jan 2024, then later tests at NASA Marshall reaching **74 psi**) exceeded NASA's safety factor by 22–27%. LIFE 285 is about 285 m³ and LIFE 500 about 500 m³. A standalone pathfinder has been proposed for **"as soon as end of 2026"** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_Integrated_Flexible_Environment), [Live Science burst video](https://www.livescience.com/space/space-exploration/watch-balloon-like-space-station-module-explode-on-purpose-during-1st-full-scale-burst-test)). The NASA FSH (§1b) and SOM Moon Village (§4) also use inflatables.
- **Look:** a **tall white "marshmallow" cylinder**. The outer surface is a **criss-cross basket-weave of straps** under a smooth white MMOD and thermal blanket. It has three internal floors, a rigid metal core and end-caps, and small round windows. On the Moon it would be **bermed with regolith** or bagged.
- Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/ef/NASA_HQ_New_A-Suite_Visit_%28KSC-20210419-PH-KLS01_0202%29.jpg (LIFE mock-up in the NASA HQ visit set) · burst test video via [Universe Today](https://www.universetoday.com/167957/watch-an-inflatable-habitat-burst-in-super-slo-mo)

## 14. Communications: Queqiao-2, Gateway (paused), Moonlight, LunaNet

- **Queqiao-2 (鹊桥二号, "Magpie Bridge 2"):** launched 20 Mar 2024. It carries a **4.2 m umbrella-like parabolic dish**, the largest on any deep-space exploration satellite, on a ~1,200 kg bus with 1,350 W solar wings. It flies a 24-hour elliptical "frozen" lunar orbit and relays for Chang'e-6 (done), **Chang'e-7** and **Chang'e-8**. It deployed the small **Tiandu-1/2** nav/comm test satellites. *Name easter egg:* 鹊桥 is the **magpie bridge** of the Qixi (Cowherd and Weaver Girl) legend, a bridge that reunites separated lovers. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queqiao-2)). Image: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8e/Polrelais.jpg
- **Lunar Gateway:** **paused 24 Mar 2026**. Don't show it as an operating station in a 2030s story unless it's a deliberate alternate history. ESA's already-built hardware (HALO was delivered in 2025, I-Hab) is being repurposed. Image (for reference only): https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/3c/Lunar_Gateway_rendering_2.webp
- **ESA Moonlight:** **Lunar Pathfinder** (SSTL) relay starts service around 2026. The full Moonlight constellation (1 comms plus 4 nav satellites) is targeted for initial operations by the end of 2028 and full operations by 2030, interoperable with NASA's **LCRNS** under **LunaNet** ("lunar internet and GPS") ([ESA](https://www.esa.int/Newsroom/Press_Releases/ESA_launches_Moonlight_to_establish_lunar_communications_and_navigation_infrastructure)).
- **Surface look:**
  - **Tall lattice towers on ridges** (SOM's plan, and Nokia's 4G/LTE lunar network concept), with dish antennas **aimed nearly horizontally** at Earth because Earth sits on the south-pole horizon.
  - Direct-to-Earth links only work while Earth is above the local horizon. At the best south-pole sites that is only **about 50% of the time** ([arXiv 2406.19863](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.19863)), which is why relays exist.
  - **Story beat:** a dropped video call when Earth dips below the ridge is *real physics*.

## 15. Synthesis: an art-direction blueprint for "广寒 Guanghan Station"

A plausible south-pole base circa 2032, with every element traceable to the sources above:

1. **Site:** a sunlit ridge crest (like the Connecting Ridge) on the rim of a black crater (Shackleton). Earth sits low on the horizon in one fixed direction, roughly toward the 0° meridian (see the Experiments doc, §10).
2. **Power:**
   - **Vertical solar arrays on 10–20 m masts** that slowly rotate to follow a sun circling the horizon once every ~29.5 days.
   - A **fission reactor** about a kilometre away, behind a berm, with tall radiator fins.
   - **Cables** on the ground.
3. **Habitats:**
   - One **vertical inflatable "Foundation" stack**, 6.5 m wide and three storeys, with an observation window.
   - **SOM-style pods** joined by short tunnels.
   - **Regolith berms or sandbags** half-burying everything.
   - **Laser-sintered dark glassy ribbed shells** (ICON/BIG) for newer buildings, with **printed-brick walls** being stacked by a robot (Chang'e-8 style).
4. **Mobility:** two **open LTVs**, a **pressurized "Lunar Cruiser"** with a rear suitport, graded roads, and survey drones (MoonFall) hopping between ridges.
5. **Landing zone:** a **glassy sintered pad** with a berm, about 1–2 km away, with a **Starship HLS or Blue Moon** towering on it. Its skyscraper scale against the low base is a great shot.
6. **Comms:** a **lattice tower** on the highest point, with dishes aimed at the horizon toward Earth.
7. **Science:** a **seismometer** station in a shaded pit, a **retroreflector** panel, a **plant chamber** with a pink-purple LED glow inside the hab, an **ice-prospecting rover** with headlights at the edge of a PSR, and an **ISRU plant** (a glowing molten-regolith reactor inside an insulated vessel, with O₂ tanks).
8. **Palette:**
   - **Neutral graphite-gray** regolith (lighter on highlands), **pure black** sky and shadows, **white and silver** hardware.
   - **Gold and amber MLI foil** on landers. **Dark glassy** sintered surfaces.
   - The only saturated color in the landscape is the **blue-white Earth** on the horizon, which fits the treatment's "color = home" rule. Everything else (gold MLI foil, red suit stripes, warm interior light) is hardware or human-made.
