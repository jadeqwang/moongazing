### The pad
- The pad is a **3x3 grid**. Four panels have arrows: **Up (top middle), Down (bottom middle), Left (middle left), Right (middle right)**. The **centre panel is blank and does not register**. The corners carry no arrows. On home soft pads, the top corners usually hold the Back/Start (or X/O) buttons.
- A **home soft pad** is a thin vinyl mat about 90 cm (3 ft) square, printed with bright arrows. It wrinkles and slides on hard floors, so people tape it down or put it on carpet. A **metal home pad** or arcade pad is a raised platform 3-5 cm thick with steel panels and an acrylic-covered arrow on each. Home pads have **no safety bar**. Arcade cabinets have a bar behind the player.

### Correct body mechanics, as good players do it
- **Stance:** face the screen with your hips square to it. The feet start on the **centre panel**, or straddle it with one foot on Left and one on Right. Good players **do not go back to centre between steps**. Each foot stays on the last arrow it hit, which keeps movement to a minimum.
- **Weight and knees:** the knees stay soft and slightly bent and the torso stays upright. Players step **lightly**, with the heel down on most steps (toes for Up), pushing downward rather than sideways, with as little airtime and lift as possible ("step as lightly as you can get away with").
- **Bounce:** casual and intermediate players bob slightly on the beat through the knees. Experts look quieter: the upper body stays still and the legs do the work.
- **Arms:** at home there is no bar, so the arms hang loose and swing naturally for balance, sometimes held slightly out to the side or with elbows a little bent. Arcade experts often hold the bar behind them.
- **Eyes:** the eyes stay **on the screen, not on the feet**. Arrows scroll **upward** toward a row of four grey target arrows at the top of the screen. A step should land when a coloured arrow overlaps its target. Beginners glance down to find their position again. Getting better means learning to feel the panels with your feet.
- **Footwork:** feet **alternate** left-right-left. "Double-stepping" (the same foot twice) is a beginner habit. A **jump** means two arrows at once, usually Left+Right or Up+Down, and you land with both feet together on both panels. Crossovers happen when, say, the left foot crosses in front to hit Right, with the hips turning slightly.
- **Classic beginner mistakes:** looking down at the feet, stomping too hard (which is tiring, loud and slows the next step), hopping back to centre after every arrow, stiff locked knees, stepping too early on the off-beat, and stepping on the dead centre or the edge of a panel instead of the arrow.

### How an adult teaches a kid (practical, general practice)
- The usual setup is **two home pads side by side** in versus or double mode, the adult on the 2P pad to the kid's right. The adult calls out "left... right... jump!" and the kid mirrors the adult's legs out of the corner of their eye. With only one pad, the adult stands **behind and slightly to one side** of the kid, off the pad, hands hovering near the kid's shoulders or waist for balance. The adult points at the screen ("watch the arrows, not your feet") and claps or bobs to the beat.
- Kids start on Beginner difficulty, which uses mostly quarter notes and single arrows.
- **What the kid looks like:** eyes wide and locked on the screen, **tongue poking out** of the corner of the mouth, arms flailing or held out like a tightrope walker, knees too straight or too bent. The kid stomps with flat feet, hair bounces, and sometimes they look down at the mat and then quickly back up. Missing a jump often ends in a giggle.
- **What the dad looks like:** a tall man (6'1") who is slightly crouched, either to match the kid's height or simply in a soft-knee stance. He is relaxed, light on his feet, upper body quiet, and grinning sideways at the kid while his feet still hit arrows. His body says he played in arcades in the early 2000s.

## (b) Astronaut family reunions after a mission

### NASA (ISS by Soyuz or Dragon; Artemis II)
- **Soyuz era:** the crew lands on the Kazakh steppe and is carried from the capsule to recliner chairs and a medical tent. On a stand-and-walk test, Scott Kelly "stood, walked, jumped, navigated obstacles". NASA crew then fly on a NASA jet from Karaganda to **Ellington Field, Houston**. They usually arrive **about 24 hours after landing, often at night**. Kelly arrived just before 2:30 a.m. EST, cleared customs and met his family **planeside or in the hangar**: two daughters, his partner, his twin brother Mark and Gabrielle Giffords. Kelly then went home to Houston that night. https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/national-international/yearlong-spacemen-embrace-fresh-air-back-on-earth-scott-kelly-houston/2022299/ ; https://www.universetoday.com/127667/scott-kelly-back-on-earth/
- **Dragon era:** after splashdown (Crew-9, 2025, off Tallahassee), the crew go to the recovery ship, then fly to Ellington Field, then to JSC crew quarters and medical checks. Families meet them at Ellington or quarters. https://space.com/space-exploration/international-space-station/never-stuck-starliner-astronauts-return-to-earth-at-last-with-crew-9-duo-in-spacex-dragon-splashdown ; https://www.nasa.gov/image-detail/amf-jsc2025e032540/
- **Artemis II (actual, 2026):** launched 1 April 2026 and splashed down in the Pacific off San Diego on 10 April. The crew were recovered and flown to Houston. On **Saturday afternoon, less than 24 hours after splashdown, at an Ellington Field hangar**, there was "a quick reunion with their spouses and children". Then Wiseman, Glover, Koch and Hansen took a **hangar stage** in front of hundreds of JSC workers and guests for a welcome-home ceremony. https://www.sanjuandailystar.com/post/artemis-ii-crew-reunites-with-families-and-fellow-nasa-astronauts ; https://spectrumlocalnews.com/ca/california/human-interest/2026/04/11/artemis-ii-s-moon-traveling-astronauts-return-home
- **Rehabilitation:** NASA's ASCR reconditioning programme runs **2 hours a day, 7 days a week, for 45 days**, starting on landing day. It covers mobility and flexibility first, then proprioception and cardio, then functional training. https://ianslive.in/after-return-to-earth-sunita-williams-undergoes-rehabilitation--20250319092653 ; https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20110020318
- **Physiology after about a year (Kelly, 340 days):** at first his legs felt like "foreign tree stumps", with blood pooling and swelling and pain on standing. He got up "like an old man getting out of a recliner" and had a rash wherever his skin touched the bed. He said readapting to gravity was harder than the year in space. https://www.iflscience.com/space/scott-kelly-shares-experiences-of-struggling-to-walk-after-year-in-space/

### China (Shenzhou)
- The capsule lands at the **Dongfeng landing site in Inner Mongolia**. Crews are carried out to chairs, then usually **fly to Beijing within hours, often arriving the same night or early the next morning**. Examples: Shenzhou-17 landed at 17:46 and arrived in Beijing "early on Wednesday". Shenzhou-21 (210 days) landed on a Friday evening and was in Beijing on Saturday. https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202405/01/content_WS66317e49c6d0868f4e8e69b5.html ; https://english.henan.gov.cn/2026/05-30/3360068.html
- **Arrival:** at Xijiao airport or **Beijing Aerospace City**, families, teammates and researchers welcome the crew with flowers and music. This is a **brief** reunion. https://www.space.com/china-shenzhou-14-astronauts-recovery-on-earth
- **Recovery has three phases:** isolation recovery (隔离恢复, about 2-3 weeks, reported as "three to four weeks" for some crews), then recuperation (疗养恢复), then observation (恢复观察). The whole programme takes about 3-6 months. Crews make their **first public appearance about two months after landing**. For example, Shenzhou-18 landed on 4 November 2024 and appeared on 8 January 2025. https://www.news.cn/tech/20250108/d25a441b06f54c588133a6f719d67a04/c.html
- **Example of a mother returning:** Wang Yaping (Shenzhou-13, 183 days) later had a reported reunion with her daughter **in a small park at Beijing Aerospace City**, where they sang together. https://www.chinanews.com.cn/sh/2025/10-13/10497509.shtml (search result; not independently verified)
